# Service pages — what Branham needs to supply

Four service pages are live:

- `/services/preconstruction`
- `/services/construction-management`
- `/services/epc`
- `/services/design-build`

Each has its own title, meta description, H1, `Service` + `BreadcrumbList`
schema, OG image, sitemap entry, and links from the homepage and footer.

## The honest limitation

Every sentence on these pages traces back to something Branham has already
published — the homepage Services section, the Hero statistics, the About copy,
or a case study. Nothing was invented to fill space.

That keeps the pages accurate, and it makes them **thin**: roughly 300 words
each. Pages that rank for competitive terms like "EPC contractor South
Carolina" generally run 800–1,500 words with specifics a competitor cannot
copy. Padding them with generic industry language would add length without
adding anything a buyer or a search engine values.

The fix is real detail. Each item below would materially strengthen a page.

### All four pages

| Needed | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Licence numbers and states held | Commercial buyers check this before shortlisting, and it is a direct trust signal |
| Certifications (OSHA, NABCEP, LEED AP, trade bodies) | Differentiates from contractors who cannot claim them |
| Bonding capacity | Often the first filter on public and institutional work |
| Typical project size range, in dollars or MW | Helps the right enquiries self-select and saves sales time |
| Insurance limits | Routinely requested at prequalification |

### Per page

**Preconstruction** — how far ahead of construction the team typically engages;
whether estimating is offered as a standalone service or only with delivery;
examples of value engineering that changed a project's cost.

**Construction Management** — safety record (EMR or incident rate, if
favourable); whether work is self-performed or subcontracted and in what
proportion; typical crew sizes; the commissioning standard followed.

**EPC** — in-house engineering disciplines and whether any are subcontracted;
PE licensure by state; procurement relationships or distribution agreements;
total installed capacity delivered to date.

**Design-Build** — whether architecture is in-house or partnered; design-build
projects completed; a real before/after on schedule compression against
design-bid-build.

## One claim to verify

The homepage states **"45+ Years of Excellence"**, **"100+ Projects
Delivered"**, and **"2nd Gen Family-Owned Business"**. These were already
published and are repeated on the service pages. If any has drifted out of
date, correcting it on the homepage updates it everywhere.

## Deliberately not done

An external audit recommended two items that were declined:

**`FAQPage` schema.** Google retired FAQ rich results from Search on 7 May
2026 and removed the documentation that June. The markup would render nothing.
There is also no Q&A content on the site, so it would have meant writing
questions purely to carry the markup.

**`Review` / `AggregateRating` schema.** Google's review snippet policy makes
the reviewed entity's own pages ineligible for star ratings — self-serving
reviews do not produce rich results. There is no manual-action risk, but no
benefit either. The site also carries one testimonial (Eric McClam, City Roots
Farm) and no numeric rating, so populating `AggregateRating` would have meant
inventing a score.

If Branham wants star ratings in search results, the route is genuine reviews
on Google Business Profile, which already shows 506 profile views and 275
customer interactions. Those appear in the local pack and the knowledge panel,
which is where buyers actually look.
